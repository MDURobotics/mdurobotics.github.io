## The problem

A robot arm that helps a person must understand what the person *wants* to do, and it must never do anything dangerous. The goal was to build an arm that can grasp a cup, move it and release it, controlled by the user's own muscle signals.

## How it works

- **Sense** EMG sensors read the weak electrical signals produced in the muscles when you tense them. The team built their own electronics to get the signals clear enough. Motion sensors follow the arm's position, a depth camera looks for objects and pressure sensors in the hand feel when it is holding something.
- **Plan** Small microcontrollers (ESP32) interpret the signals and decide whether the user wants to grip or release.
- **Act** Motors in the 3D-printed arm and hand make the movement.
{: .sta}

## How did it go?

The muscle sensors worked best. In every trial it was clearly possible to tell a resting muscle from a gripping one, with a little noise. The motion sensors and the camera worked as demonstrations but had problems, for example when something blocked the camera. The arm is being used and improved by next year's team.

## Words to know

- **EMG:** electromyography, measuring the muscles' electrical signals through the skin.
- **Bionic:** a technical construction that imitates a body part.
- **Noise:** disturbances in a signal that make it harder to interpret, a bit like crackling on a radio.

**Think about:** The brain sends signals to the muscles before you have time to move. How could a robot arm use that?
