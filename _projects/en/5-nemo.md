## The problem

Self-driving boats can be used in rescue operations, to monitor the environment or to patrol coasts. But water is tricky: waves, currents and wind constantly push the boat off course, the electronics must withstand water, and the camera can be fooled by reflections on the surface. This is MDU's first self-driving motorboat, so the team is starting from a blank sheet.

## How it works

- **Sense** GPS tells where the boat is, motion sensors how it tilts and turns, and cameras facing forward and backward see obstacles and markers.
- **Plan** A computer on board plans a route between points and around obstacles.
- **Act** Motors and propellers steer the boat. The competition events are manoeuvring, docking, avoiding collisions and finding the way.
{: .sta}

**The competition's safety rules.** The boat must have an emergency switch both on board and on the remote control, and turn itself off if radio contact has been lost for 60 seconds. The power supply must sit in a fire box that withstands flames for 4 minutes. The boat may weigh at most 65 kg so that three people can launch it, and must run at 1–5 knots. A light visible from 100 metres shows the mode: 🔴 red = emergency stop, 🟡 yellow = remote-controlled, 🟢 green = driving itself.
{: .callout}

## Words to know

- **ASV:** Autonomous Surface Vehicle, a vessel that drives itself on the water surface.
- **Metacentric height:** a measure of how stable a boat is. Too small and it capsizes, too large and it becomes stiff and jerky in waves.
- **Catamaran:** a boat with two narrow hulls side by side, which makes it stable.

**Think about:** Why is it harder to make a boat keep a straight course than a car? Think about what happens when you stop pedalling a bike compared with when you stop paddling a canoe.
